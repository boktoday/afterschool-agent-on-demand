Part 2 — The Three Rituals

Three things the agent does every single day.

Over four weeks, your teen configures their agent to perform three daily rituals. By the end, these run automatically — the agent greets them after school, helps them plan, and speaks a one-minute morning briefing when they wake up.

Ritual 1 · After School · 4:30 PM

The Afterschool Chat

Your teen sits down, opens their agent, and talks. How was the day? What's weighing on them? What's the plan tonight? The agent listens, reflects, and helps them decompress before screen time takes over. It's a grounding conversation — the essential of silence and real community in practice.

teen:hey, school was okay. kind of tired. got a maths test thursday.
agent:good to hear from you. let's be real — tired and a test on thursday. this week's essential is stillness. want to try one minute of quiet before we plan, or do you need to talk first?
teen:maybe a minute.
agent:alright. timer starts now. one minute of stillness. i'll wait.
agent:…
agent:welcome back. how was that? now let's look at thursday — want me to draft a study plan, 30 minutes tuesday, 45 wednesday, review thursday morning?
teen:yeah, and can you make the morning minute about the test?
agent:done. your morning minute tomorrow will open with: "thursday's your maths test. you've prepared. take one breath. then trust the work." and we'll try another stillness minute at the end — that's your Essential 1 challenge for today.
Ritual 2 · Evening · Anytime, Self-paced

The Planning Session

Before bed, the agent helps them map tomorrow. School tasks, homework deadlines, the essential they're working on this week, one real-world thing they want to do. The agent drafts the plan, your teen edits it, and it saves locally. No app, no account, no cloud sync — just their agent remembering for them.

Ritual 3 · Every Morning · Auto-generated

The Morning Minute

This is the magic. Each morning, the agent generates a one-minute text-to-speech audio briefing — their plan, their essential for the week, a thought to carry, a breath to take. Your teen wakes up, presses play, and starts the day with intention instead of a feed. The agent builds it overnight from yesterday's plan.

Sample Morning Minute — Tuesday, Week 1

"Good morning. It's Tuesday. You have maths prep tonight — thirty minutes, just the practice paper. Yesterday's stillness went well; let's do another minute at 7 AM tomorrow. One thing to carry today: nothing has to be perfect. Done is better than perfect. Now go."

How Personalisation Works

The agent isn't reading from a script. During the live sessions, your teen tells the agent about themselves — what they like, what's hard, what their week looks like. The agent uses that to design activities that actually fit. A sporty kid gets a different Body-as-Temple challenge than a bookish one. A teen who's anxious about screens gets a gentler Digital Self-Defence ramp than one who's already cutting back. Every activity is saved locally with a short reflection. By the end of four weeks, your teen has a portfolio of eight personalised essential challenges — and an agent that knows them well enough to keep going.

Part 4 — Pick Your Tier

How the agent runs.

Before installing anything, choose how the agent will run. There are three tiers. All three do exactly the same job — the difference is where the language model lives and what it costs.

Recommended
Tier 1 — Local-First
Free · forever

Uses LM Studio + small open-source models on this computer. The conversational model is Qwen3.5 8B (or LFM2-24B-A2B on beefier hardware). The compact model is Liquid LFM2 1.2B, designed for on-device. No data leaves the device at any point.

Best for: families who want maximum privacy, no subscriptions, no internet dependency.

Tier 2 — OpenCode Go (cloud)
$5 first month, then $10/month

Uses OpenCode Go for the conversational model (Kimi K3, Grok 4.5, GLM-5, etc. — pick any from their catalogue). The compact LFM2 1.2B still runs locally for the morning-minute generator and reflection classifier. Conversations hit OpenCode's servers; everything else stays on the device.

Best for: laptops that can't run big models locally, faster conversations, parents who don't mind a small subscription.

Tier 3 — Bring Your Own
Variable · depends on provider

You already have an API key for Claude, OpenAI, OpenRouter, Grok, or any other provider Hermes supports. The local LFM2 1.2B still runs for compact tasks. Use whatever model you trust.

Best for: existing subscribers, power users, anyone who knows exactly what they want.

Why a Local Compact Model, Even in the Cloud Tier

Even on Tier 2 or Tier 3, we keep Liquid LFM2 1.2B running locally. It's used for the small tasks: generating the Morning Minute template, classifying reflections (real vs deflected), picking the right wisdom quote for the day's essential, and rewriting text for the TTS engine. These are offline-immune — if your teen's wifi drops during the afterschool chat, the local model keeps running, queues tasks, and the chat resumes seamlessly when connectivity returns.

Part 6 — Personalisation Questions

What the agent asks, and why.

During the first afterschool chat, your teen's agent walks them through a short personalisation flow. Every answer is stored locally on the device — never uploaded, never shared, deletable at any time. Here's what gets asked, and what the agent does with each answer:

Question Why we ask
What's your name? So the agent can greet you personally, refer back to you, and use your name sparingly (not every message — that gets old fast).
How old are you? Shapes tone. A 13-year-old gets different vocabulary and pacing than a 17-year-old. The agent doesn't pretend to be a peer — it adjusts how much hand-holding the conversation needs.
What's a typical day look like for you? The agent uses this to schedule the Morning Minute at the right time, suggest Planning Sessions when you're most likely to be free, and design activities that fit (e.g. walks before school if you have time, not at 11pm).
What do you actually enjoy? The agent weaves your interests into the essential challenges. A kid who loves basketball gets a Body-as-Temple challenge about rest and recovery, not just generic "drink water" reminders.
What's hard right now? The agent uses this to decide which essentials to lead with. If screen-time is the problem, Digital Self-Defence isn't just a topic — it's first in the queue. If school stress is the issue, the Morning Minute gets more grounding in week 1.
Faith-wise, where are you coming from? Shapes the wisdom quotes the agent shares. Christian / Muslim / Jewish / Buddhist / Hindu / secular humanist / Stoic / spiritual-but-not-religious / other — each path has its own library of wisdom the agent can draw from.
How do you want me to talk to you? The agent doesn't have one voice. It can be chatty, or quiet. It can use slang, or be formal. It can call you by name every message, or once at the start. This question gives your teen permission to be explicit.
Is there anything you want me to avoid? Topics, tones, metaphors — anything that doesn't land. The agent respects this for the life of the project.
For Parents

You're welcome to sit with your teen during the personalisation flow. The questions are designed to be answered by the teen themselves, not by you — but there's no rule. If you'd rather they answer alone, the agent will gently close the chat and continue the personalisation when they're ready. All answers live in ~/.hermes/state.db on their device. You can read them any time, or wipe them with a single command.

Part 8 — How to Write Your Own Skills

By week 4, you'll be writing these yourself.

Every Hermes skill has the same shape. Four pieces: frontmatter, voice, memory, and ritual hook. Once your teen knows the shape, they can write a 9th skill — for homework, for their hobby, for anything they want an agent to do.

The Shape of a Skill

---
name: homework-helper
description: Help Alex with maths and English homework.
  Doesn't give answers — asks the right questions.
---

# Homework Helper — Personal Skill for Alex

## Voice
Patient. Socratic. Never gives the answer directly —
asks one question at a time. Celebrates the moment
Alex figures it out themselves.

## Memory
- homework_current_topic: what they're working on today
- homework_stuck_on: where they got stuck last session
- homework_style: how Alex learns best (worked example,
  analogy, diagrams, step-by-step)

## Ritual hook
Runs as part of the Afterschool Chat (Ritual 1).
If Alex mentions homework, this skill takes over.

## What this skill does NOT do
- Doesn't write essays for Alex
- Doesn't do the maths for them
- Doesn't pretend to know things it doesn't

The Pattern

  1. Frontmatter. Name and one-sentence description. This is what the orchestrator sees when deciding which skill to delegate to.
  2. Voice. 2-4 sentences on tone. Not what to say, but how to say it. Be explicit. "Patient. Socratic. Never gives the answer directly."
  3. Memory. The keys this skill reads and writes. Skills are honest about what they need to remember.
  4. Ritual hook. When does this skill fire? Morning Minute? Afterschool Chat? A specific essential? Be specific.
  5. What this skill does NOT do. This is the most important section. Negative constraints are what keep agents from drifting into behaviour the teen didn't ask for.
Try It Yourself

After the four weeks, the teen should be able to write a 9th skill — maybe a guitar-practice-coach, a volunteer-log-keeper, a conversation-starter for when they're nervous at parties. Anything that fits their life. The skill system is the point. The eight essentials are the curriculum.

Part 9 — Making the Most of the Rituals

How to actually use the three rituals well.

The Afterschool Chat (4:30 PM)

  • Don't skip days. The agent learns you from these chats. Every skipped day is data the agent doesn't have. Even five minutes counts.
  • Be specific. "School was okay" gives the agent less to work with than "Maths was fine but English was rough — we got a new teacher and she's intense." Specificity unlocks personalisation.
  • Let the agent finish. It will sometimes ask a follow-up or notice something you didn't say. That's not nagging — it's the reflection pattern working.
  • Push back. If the agent gives advice you didn't ask for, say so. "I just wanted to vent" is a valid response. The agent recalibrates.

The Planning Session (Evening)

  • Run it the night before, not the morning of. The Morning Minute is generated from the plan. If the plan doesn't exist, the minute has nothing to work with.
  • One real-world thing. Not "be healthier" — "walk to school tomorrow instead of getting a lift." The agent will remind you of the walk in the morning.
  • Edit the agent's draft. It will propose tasks. You decide which ones are real. This is the part that makes the plan yours.
  • Keep it short. 3-5 tasks is plenty. The point isn't productivity theatre — it's actually knowing what tomorrow looks like.

The Morning Minute (Auto-generated)

  • Press play before you touch your phone. This is the moment. The first thing you hear shapes the day. Let the agent have it.
  • One breath at the end. The agent builds a pause into the audio. Use it.
  • If the minute feels wrong, edit the plan. The Morning Minute is downstream. If you don't like what it says, change the inputs the night before.
  • Skip days with intention, not by accident. If you skip it, log why. "Was rushing" is a useful answer. "Forgot" is a habit worth breaking.
The Two-Minute Rule

If the agent asks something that would take less than two minutes to do right now (try a breath, name one thing you're grateful for, write down the time), do it now. The reflection isn't about the response — it's about the act of pausing long enough to respond. The longer you put it off, the less the practice sticks.

Part 10 — Safety & Privacy

Where the data lives, who can see it, and how to wipe it.

What gets stored

  • Personalisation answers (name, age, day shape, faith posture, what to avoid) — stored in ~/.hermes/state.db on the device.
  • Conversation history — stored in ~/.hermes/sessions/ as JSONL transcripts. Local. Plain text. Searchable with grep.
  • Plans and morning minutes — stored as Markdown files in ~/.hermes/projects/one-to-eight/plans/.
  • Skill files — stored in ~/.hermes/skills/productivity/<skill-name>/.
  • Nothing else. No telemetry, no analytics, no phone-home.

Who can see it

  • Tier 1 (Local): Only people with access to the device. If the teen has their own laptop, that's them. If it's a shared family computer, everyone.
  • Tier 2 (OpenCode Go): Conversations are sent to OpenCode's servers. OpenCode's privacy policy applies. Skills, plans, and personalisation answers still stay local.
  • Tier 3 (BYO): Whatever the provider's policy is. Read it before you choose.

How to wipe everything

# Wipe all session history
rm -rf ~/.hermes/sessions/*

# Wipe personalisation memory
rm -rf ~/.hermes/state.db

# Wipe everything (full reset)
rm -rf ~/.hermes/

# Or, if you want to keep the install but lose the teen's data:
hermes memory clear --target user

For Parents — Quick Sanity Checks

  • Read the session files. They're plain text. Open ~/.hermes/sessions/ in any text editor. Skim. You'll see everything your teen has said to the agent.
  • Read the skill files. ~/.hermes/skills/productivity/. The skill files contain the personalisation answers and the agent's voice instructions. They're not encrypted.
  • Watch the model. If your teen is on Tier 2, you can see in the terminal when conversations are being sent to OpenCode's servers. The agent doesn't hide this.

Does my teen need to know how to code?
No. Hermes is a natural-language agent platform — your teen configures it by talking to it and writing plain-English instructions in skill files. We teach everything step by step in the live sessions. If they can use a chat app, they can build an agent.
What equipment do we need?
A laptop or desktop computer (Windows, Mac, or Linux) with internet access for the live webcast. That's it. The Hermes software is free and runs locally.
